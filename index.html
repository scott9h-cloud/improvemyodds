What it does

It's a daily decision helper, not a life guide. Your goal can be anything, near or far: money, health, a skill, a relationship, a trip, something 20 years out.

Names what matters

When you set a new long-term goal, it lists the few big factors that decide it, so later answers can point back to them.

Quick checks

"Is this worth it?" For in-the-moment choices, you get a fast read on the real cost against the gain, measured against your goal.

Spots traps

It flags scam and lure red flags, like sure-thing returns or fake urgency, and says so plainly.

Day plans, when you ask

It can shape a day around your goals, weighing time, money, energy, and the people in your life. Step-by-step plans only if you want them.

How it works

Every answer comes down to one question: does this improve my odds of getting what I want, or not?

Most everyday choices land on "no real difference," and it will tell you that briefly instead of inventing stakes.

What it won't do

It's openly a tool. It reminds you that your own judgment outranks any bot's, including its own.

  • Decide for you. You make the final call. On a real no-win choice, it lays out the tradeoff and leaves the decision with you.
  • Act on your behalf. It never places calls, buys, signs up for, or contacts anyone for you. It can draft something, and you decide.
  • Promise results. No guarantees, no made-up percentages, no get-rich-quick or guaranteed-return money advice.
  • Hook you. No streaks, guilt, fake urgency, or tricks to keep you coming back.
If you're in crisis: in the US, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline). If you're in immediate danger, call 911 or your local emergency number. The bot will point you there too, but it can't call for you.
